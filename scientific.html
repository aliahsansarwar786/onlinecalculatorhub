How to Use the Scientific Calculator

  1. Trigonometry (Degrees): Press sin, cos, or tan followed by the angle in degrees, and close with ). For instance, sin(30) = 0.5.
  2. Powers and Exponents: Enter the base, click x^y (which inserts ^), and enter the exponent. For squaring, click x^2.
  3. Roots: Press √ to insert sqrt(, enter the radicand, and close the bracket.
  4. Logarithms: Use log for common logarithms (base 10) or ln for natural logarithms (base e).
  5. Factorials: Enter a non-negative integer followed by n! to calculate factorials.
  6. Constants: Use the π (∼3.14159) and e (∼2.71828) buttons directly in your formulas.

Mathematical Formulas & Definitions

The mathematical relationships governing the functions are detailed below:

Trigonometry (Deg) : sin(θ°) = sin(θ × π / 180 rad) Power : x^y = x multiplied by itself y times Square Root : √x = x^(1/2), where x ≥ 0 Common Logarithm : log10(x) = y ⇔ 10^y = x Natural Logarithm : ln(x) = y ⇔ e^y = x Factorial : n! = n × (n - 1) × (n - 2) × ... × 1 (with 0! = 1)

Frequently Asked Questions

Are trigonometric functions evaluated in degrees or radians?

By default and design, all trigonometric functions (sin, cos, tan) on OnlineCalculatorHub are calculated in degrees. For example, sin(30) calculates as 0.5.

What is the difference between log10 and ln?

log10 calculates the common logarithm with base 10 (e.g., log10(100) = 2). In contrast, ln calculates the natural logarithm using Euler's number e (approx 2.71828) as the base (e.g., ln(e) = 1).

How does the factorial (n!) function work?

Factorial multiplies an integer by every positive integer below it. For example, 5! = 5 * 4 * 3 * 2 * 1 = 120. Factorials for negative numbers or non-integers return an Error.

How do I calculate custom powers like 2^3?

Use the x^y button to insert the exponentiation operator (^). For example, entering 2^3 and pressing '=' yields 8.
